## Phase 1 — Triage & Signal Quality

> Before doing anything, determine whether the event is signal or noise.

### Source Credibility
- Is this from a primary source (regulatory filing, official press release, central bank statement)?
- Is this from a Tier-1 financial outlet (Reuters, Bloomberg, FT, WSJ)?
- Is this from social media or an unverified source?
- Has a second credible source confirmed it?
- Is the timing suspicious (pre-market, after-hours, Friday afternoon — historically when bad news is buried)?
- Is this a rumor, a leak, or confirmed fact?

### Event Scale
- Does this affect one company, a sector, or the entire system?
- Is the entity involved systemically important (SIFI — Systemically Important Financial Institution)?
- What is the size of the entity relative to GDP, total market cap, or credit markets?
- Does this event cross a critical threshold (e.g., a credit rating downgrade from investment grade to junk)?

### Reversibility
- Is this event structural (bankruptcy, policy change, regulatory ruling) or temporary (supply disruption, management scandal)?
- Can the affected entity recover within one quarter, one year, or never?
- Is there a government backstop, bailout mechanism, or central bank intervention likely?

### Speed of Propagation
- How fast is this spreading across markets (seconds, hours, days)?
- Are correlated assets already moving?
- Is there contagion risk to counterparties?

### Information Edge Assessment
- Is this already fully priced in?
- What does the market not yet know?
- What is our information advantage, if any?

## Phase 2 — Macroeconomic Parameters

> The backdrop determines whether a shock is absorbed or amplified.

### Interest Rate Environment
- Current policy rate (Fed Funds, ECB rate, BoE rate, etc.)
- Direction of travel: hiking cycle, cutting cycle, or on hold?
- Real interest rate (nominal rate minus inflation)
- Yield curve shape: normal, flat, inverted?
- Yield curve steepness (10Y minus 2Y spread)
- Rate expectations: what does the futures market imply for next 12 months?
- Terminal rate consensus

### Inflation
- Current CPI (headline and core)
- PCE (Fed's preferred measure)
- PPI (input cost pressure)
- Inflation trend: accelerating, decelerating, or anchored?
- Inflation expectations (5Y5Y breakeven, University of Michigan survey)
- Wage growth (Average Hourly Earnings, Employment Cost Index)
- Rent/shelter inflation (stickiest component)

### Growth
- GDP growth rate (current and forecast)
- GDP composition: consumption, investment, government, net exports
- PMI (Manufacturing and Services — leading indicator)
- ISM Manufacturing and Non-Manufacturing
- Industrial Production
- Retail Sales (real, not nominal)
- Consumer Confidence (Conference Board, University of Michigan)
- Durable Goods Orders
- Leading Economic Index (LEI)
- Recession probability models (e.g., NY Fed recession probability)

### Employment
- Non-Farm Payrolls (headline and private sector)
- Unemployment rate (U3 and U6 — includes discouraged and part-time workers)
- Labor Force Participation Rate
- JOLTS (Job Openings, Quits Rate — forward signal)
- Initial and Continuing Jobless Claims
- ADP Employment Report
- Wage growth by sector

### Credit Conditions
- Investment Grade (IG) credit spreads (OAS)
- High Yield (HY) credit spreads — key risk-on/risk-off gauge
- Leveraged Loan spreads
- TED Spread (T-bill vs LIBOR/SOFR — interbank stress gauge)
- LIBOR-OIS spread (banking sector stress)
- Commercial and Industrial Loan growth
- Senior Loan Officer Survey (bank lending standards tightening/easing?)
- Delinquency rates (auto, mortgage, credit card, commercial real estate)

### Liquidity
- M2 money supply growth (year-over-year)
- Federal Reserve balance sheet size and trajectory (QE vs QT)
- Reserve levels in banking system
- Repo market conditions (any stress in overnight lending?)
- Dollar funding conditions globally (FX swap basis)
- Global central bank balance sheet aggregate

### Currency
- DXY (US Dollar Index) — stronger dollar tightens global financial conditions
- EUR/USD, USD/JPY, USD/CNY (key cross rates)
- Emerging market currency basket
- Currency volatility (implied vol from FX options)
- Safe-haven flows (CHF, JPY, gold appreciation signals stress)

### Commodity Prices
- Crude oil (WTI and Brent) — energy costs, inflation, geopolitical signal
- Natural gas
- Copper (economic bellwether — "Dr. Copper")
- Gold (fear gauge, inflation hedge, dollar signal)
- Silver
- Agricultural commodities (food inflation signal)
- Lumber (housing activity signal)
- Baltic Dry Index (global trade volume)

## Phase 3 — Market Structure Parameters

> Understanding how markets are positioned before the event.

### Equity Markets
- Index levels and year-to-date performance (S&P 500, Nasdaq, Russell 2000, MSCI World, EM)
- Sector rotation trends
- Growth vs Value spread
- Small cap vs large cap performance
- Market breadth: advance-decline ratio, percentage of stocks above 200-day MA
- New 52-week highs vs lows
- Price/Earnings ratio (trailing and forward P/E)
- Price/Sales, Price/Book, EV/EBITDA
- Earnings yield vs bond yield spread (equity risk premium)
- Dividend yield
- Buyback yield

### Volatility
- VIX (S&P 500 30-day implied volatility) — fear gauge
- VVIX (volatility of VIX — second-order fear)
- MOVE Index (bond market volatility)
- Term structure of volatility (contango vs backwardation)
- Realized vs implied volatility (vol risk premium)
- Put/Call ratio (equity options market sentiment)
- Skew (cost of downside protection relative to upside)

### Positioning & Flows
- CFTC Commitment of Traders (COT) — speculative positioning in futures
- Fund flows (into/out of equity, bond, money market funds)
- ETF flows
- Short interest levels
- Margin debt levels
- Risk parity fund positioning
- CTA (Commodity Trading Advisor) trend-following positioning
- Options gamma exposure (market maker positioning)

### Fixed Income
- 2Y, 5Y, 10Y, 30Y Treasury yields
- Real yields (TIPS yields)
- Breakeven inflation rates
- Municipal bond spreads
- Mortgage-backed securities spreads
- Agency spreads

## Phase 4 — Company / Entity Parameters

> Applied when the event involves a specific company.

### Financial Health
- Revenue growth (year-over-year, quarter-over-quarter)
- Gross margin and trend
- EBITDA margin
- Net profit margin
- Free cash flow (FCF) and FCF yield
- Cash and cash equivalents on balance sheet
- Total debt (gross and net of cash)
- Debt/EBITDA ratio (leverage)
- Interest coverage ratio (EBIT / interest expense)
- Debt maturity schedule (near-term refinancing risk?)
- Current ratio (current assets / current liabilities)
- Quick ratio (liquid assets / current liabilities)
- Days Sales Outstanding (DSO — receivables quality)
- Days Inventory Outstanding (DIO)
- Days Payable Outstanding (DPO)
- Cash conversion cycle

### Business Quality
- Market share (and trend)
- Pricing power (ability to pass through cost increases)
- Customer concentration (top 10 customers as % of revenue)
- Geographic revenue diversification
- Product/service diversification
- Switching costs for customers
- Brand strength
- Regulatory moat
- Patent protection
- Network effects
- Barriers to entry in core markets

### Management & Governance
- CEO tenure and track record
- Management team depth
- Board composition and independence
- Insider ownership (aligned with shareholders?)
- Recent insider buying or selling
- Executive compensation structure (incentive alignment)
- History of capital allocation decisions
- M&A track record (value-creating or destructive?)
- Accounting quality (conservative or aggressive recognition?)
- Audit firm quality and any qualified opinions
- Related-party transactions
- ESG controversies

### Operational Parameters
- Capacity utilization
- Order backlog
- Book-to-bill ratio (tech/industrial)
- Same-store sales growth (retail)
- RevPAR (hospitality)
- Churn rate / Net Revenue Retention (SaaS)
- Customer Acquisition Cost (CAC) and Lifetime Value (LTV)
- Headcount and hiring trends
- Supply chain concentration risk (single-source suppliers?)
- Inventory levels (excess = markdown risk)
- Capital expenditure cycle (heavy investment or harvest phase?)

### Valuation
- Price/Earnings (trailing and forward)
- EV/EBITDA vs sector peers
- Price/Free Cash Flow
- PEG ratio (P/E relative to growth)
- Price/Book vs historical
- Sum-of-the-parts analysis (for conglomerates)
- DCF implied growth rate (what does the current price assume?)
- Comparable transactions multiples
- Dividend discount model implied return

### Competitive Dynamics
- Who benefits from this company's distress?
- Is a competitor likely to gain market share?
- Are there acquisition implications (takeover target/acquirer)?
- Is there an activist investor angle?

## Phase 5 — Banking & Financial Institution Parameters

> Specific lens for bank or financial institution events.

### Capital Adequacy
- CET1 ratio (Common Equity Tier 1) — core capital buffer
- Tier 1 Capital Ratio
- Total Capital Ratio
- Leverage Ratio (Tier 1 / Total Assets)
- Stress test results (Fed DFAST, EBA stress tests)
- Capital buffer above regulatory minimums
- GSIB surcharge (for global systemically important banks)

### Asset Quality
- Non-Performing Loan (NPL) ratio
- NPL coverage ratio (loan loss reserves vs NPLs)
- Net charge-off rate
- Delinquency rates by loan category (commercial, residential, consumer)
- Provision for Credit Losses (building or releasing?)
- Criticized and classified loan levels
- Commercial Real Estate (CRE) exposure — especially office
- Loan-to-Value ratios on real estate book
- Concentration risk by sector and geography

### Liquidity
- Liquidity Coverage Ratio (LCR) — 30-day stress test
- Net Stable Funding Ratio (NSFR) — longer-term funding stability
- High Quality Liquid Assets (HQLA)
- Loan-to-Deposit Ratio
- Wholesale funding dependency (commercial paper, repo, FHLB borrowings)
- Deposit composition (retail vs institutional — institutional more flighty)
- Uninsured deposit percentage (above FDIC $250K limit)
- Duration of deposit base
- Available borrowing capacity (Fed discount window, FHLB, repo lines)

### Profitability
- Net Interest Margin (NIM) and trend
- Return on Assets (ROA)
- Return on Equity (ROE) vs cost of equity
- Return on Tangible Common Equity (ROTCE)
- Efficiency ratio (non-interest expense / revenue — lower is better)
- Non-interest income as % of total revenue
- Trading revenue volatility
- Fee income growth

### Contagion Assessment
- Interbank exposure — who holds this bank's bonds or has credit lines?
- Counterparty derivatives exposure (gross and net)
- Correspondent banking relationships
- Prime brokerage clients (for investment banks)
- Money market fund exposure to the institution
- Is this institution a major repo counterparty?
- Central clearing counterparty (CCP) membership

### Regulatory & Resolution
- Is the bank under regulatory enforcement actions?
- SIFI designation — would resolution require FDIC intervention?
- Resolution plan (living will) status
- Consent orders or MRAs (Matters Requiring Attention)
- Anti-money laundering (AML) compliance record
- CFPB enforcement history (consumer banks)

## Phase 6 — Exposure Mapping

> Systematically map every link between the event and our portfolio or business.

### Direct Exposure
- Do we hold equity in the affected entity?
- Do we hold bonds issued by the entity?
- Do we have a credit facility extended to the entity?
- Do we have derivatives contracts with the entity as counterparty?
- Do we have deposits at the institution (above FDIC limits)?
- Do we have revenue dependent on the entity (as a customer)?

### Supply Chain Exposure
- Is the entity a critical input supplier?
- Do we have single-source dependency?
- What is our inventory buffer vs lead time if supply is disrupted?
- Can we qualify alternative suppliers within 30/60/90 days?
- What is the cost premium for alternative supply?

### Customer Concentration
- Is the entity one of our top 10 customers?
- What % of our revenue does this entity represent?
- What is their payment terms and current receivables balance?
- Are they likely to reduce orders or delay payment?

### Correlated Asset Exposure
- Do we hold other companies in the same sector that will be affected?
- Do we hold assets in the same geographic region?
- Do we have exposure to the same macro factor (oil price, interest rates, FX)?

### Indirect / Systemic Exposure
- If this creates credit tightening, which of our financing arrangements could be affected?
- If confidence collapses, which of our customers could face their own distress?
- Do our insurance policies cover losses related to this event?
- What is our contingent liability exposure?

### Balance Sheet Impact Assessment
- P&L impact (best/base/worst case)
- Balance sheet impact (asset impairment, write-down risk)
- Cash flow impact (timing of receivables, change in capex plans)
- Covenant headroom analysis (does this push us closer to a covenant breach?)

## Phase 7 — Scenario Construction

> Always three scenarios, probability-weighted, with specific triggers.

### Scenario Framework

| Dimension | Bear Case | Base Case | Bull Case |
|---|---|---|---|
| Probability | 20–30% | 50–60% | 15–25% |
| Duration of impact | 12–24 months | 6–12 months | 1–3 months |
| GDP impact | -1% to -3% | -0.5% to 0% | Minimal |
| Credit spread widening | +200–500 bps | +50–150 bps | +0–50 bps |
| Equity market drawdown | -20% to -40% | -5% to -15% | 0% to -5% |
| Policy response | Delayed or insufficient | Adequate | Swift and aggressive |

### Bear Case Triggers to Watch
- Policy error (central bank hikes into recession)
- Contagion to additional institutions
- Sovereign debt stress
- Credit market freeze (primary markets shut)
- Bank run dynamics (deposit flight)
- Regulatory overreaction creating feedback loop
- Geopolitical escalation compounds financial stress

### Bull Case Triggers to Watch
- Swift and credible government/central bank intervention
- Event proves contained to a single entity
- Strong economic data reduces recession fears
- Rapid resolution of the distressed entity
- Short squeeze in beaten-down assets
- Coordinated global policy response

### Historical Analogs to Reference
- 2008 Global Financial Crisis (systemic bank failure)
- 2010–2012 European Sovereign Debt Crisis
- 2020 COVID market crash and recovery
- 2023 SVB/Signature/First Republic bank failures
- 2022 UK Gilt crisis (liability-driven investment)
- Long-Term Capital Management 1998
- Asian Financial Crisis 1997
- Dot-com crash 2000–2002
- 1987 Black Monday

## Phase 8 — Behavioral & Sentiment Parameters

> Markets are as much psychology as fundamentals.

### Fear & Greed Indicators
- VIX level and term structure
- CNN Fear & Greed Index
- AAII Investor Sentiment Survey (retail bullish/bearish ratio)
- Investors Intelligence (newsletter writers bull/bear)
- Put/Call ratio (elevated = fear)
- Short interest levels
- Fund manager cash levels (BofA Global Fund Manager Survey)

### Market Narrative
- What story is the market telling itself right now?
- Is the prevailing narrative correct or is there a misunderstanding?
- Are there reflexivity dynamics (Soros — does the market reaction change fundamentals?)
- Is there a "who gets blamed" political/media narrative forming?

### Behavioral Biases to Counter
- Loss aversion: Am I holding losers too long to avoid realizing losses?
- Recency bias: Am I extrapolating the last 6 months too strongly?
- Anchoring: Am I anchored to a prior price target that is now stale?
- Herding: Am I moving with the crowd when the crowd is wrong?
- Overconfidence: Do I actually have an edge here, or do I think I do?
- Availability bias: Is this event more salient because it's recent, not because it's important?
- Confirmation bias: Am I seeking data that confirms my pre-existing view?

### Contrarian Signals
- Is everyone panicking uniformly? (contrarian buy signal)
- Is everyone complacent? (contrarian sell signal)
- Are retail investors piling in the opposite direction of institutions?
- Is short interest at extreme levels (short squeeze potential)?
- Are insiders buying while the public sells?

## Phase 9 — Geopolitical & Regulatory Parameters

> Often the slowest but most permanent forces.

### Geopolitical Risk
- Which countries/regions are involved?
- Is there military, trade, or sanction risk?
- How does this interact with existing US-China, US-Russia, NATO tensions?
- Is there energy supply disruption risk?
- Are key shipping lanes (Strait of Hormuz, Red Sea, South China Sea) affected?
- Elections calendar: which major elections are within 12 months?
- Political stability of key counterparties

### Regulatory Risk
- Which regulators have jurisdiction over this event (Fed, SEC, FDIC, FCA, ECB, etc.)?
- What is the likely regulatory response timeline?
- Is there legislation pending that this event could accelerate?
- What is the antitrust angle (if M&A is involved)?
- Is there a CFIUS (national security) review risk?
- ESG regulatory trajectory in relevant sectors
- Tax policy changes under consideration

### Legal Risk
- Class action lawsuit probability
- Regulatory fine exposure (estimate based on past precedents)
- Executive liability (criminal vs civil exposure)
- Bankruptcy process jurisdiction (US Chapter 11 vs other)
- Contract force majeure clauses (does this event trigger them?)

## Phase 10 — Action Levers & Decision Criteria

> The exact actions available, with pros and cons of each.

### Action Menu

#### 1. Hold
- **When:** High conviction, temporary noise, fundamentals intact, valuation reasonable
- **Risk:** Drawdown continues; opportunity cost if capital could be deployed better
- **Criteria to override:** If base case shifts to bear case, act; if thesis is broken, exit

#### 2. Reduce / Trim
- **When:** Uncertainty elevated, position size too large for risk tolerance, liquidity needed
- **How much:** Scale reduction to size that allows you to sleep — typically 25–50% trim
- **Risk:** Miss recovery if event resolves; tax consequences

#### 3. Exit Fully
- **When:** Thesis fundamentally broken; bear case probability > 50%; liquidity window closing
- **Risk:** Crystallizing loss; potential to miss recovery; market impact on exit

#### 4. Hedge
- **When:** Cannot exit (lock-up, tax, strategic) but want to reduce mark-to-market risk
- **Instruments:** Put options, inverse ETFs, CDS (credit default swaps), short correlated proxies, VIX calls
- **Cost:** Premium / carry cost of hedge must be weighed against protection value
- **Key question:** Is the hedge liquid enough in a crisis? (correlation goes to 1 when you need it most)

#### 5. Opportunistic Add / Buy
- **When:** Event creates mispricing; strong conviction on base case; price already discounts bear case
- **Criteria:** Margin of safety analysis; asymmetric risk/reward (2:1 or better upside/downside)
- **Risk:** Catching a falling knife; bear case has not fully played out

#### 6. Pivot / Rotate
- **When:** Sector impacted but capital can be redeployed to beneficiaries of the same trend
- **Examples:** Bank stress → long duration Treasuries; oil spike → energy producers; inflation → TIPS, commodities
- **Risk:** Correlation surprise — assumed beneficiaries also sell off in panic

#### 7. Raise Cash
- **When:** Uncertainty too high to make confident directional bets; liquidity optionality valuable
- **Risk:** Inflation erodes cash; opportunity cost if markets recover sharply
- **Tactical:** Park in short-term T-bills or money market — do not sacrifice liquidity

#### 8. Wait & Watch
- **When:** Event is too early-stage to assess; more information expected within 24–72 hours
- **Rule:** Set a specific time-limit and information trigger to force a decision

### Position Sizing Principles
- Never size a single position > 5% of portfolio in high-conviction; < 2% in speculative
- Kelly Criterion: size = edge / odds (never full Kelly — use half-Kelly or quarter-Kelly)
- Correlation-adjusted sizing: two positions that are 90% correlated count as one
- Liquidity-adjusted sizing: illiquid positions deserve a smaller weight (cannot exit easily)

## Phase 11 — Risk Management Parameters

> The discipline that keeps you in the game long enough to be right.

### Portfolio-Level Risk
- Value at Risk (VaR) — daily 95% and 99% confidence
- Conditional Value at Risk (CVaR / Expected Shortfall)
- Maximum drawdown tolerance (what % loss is acceptable before strategy review?)
- Sharpe ratio impact
- Sortino ratio (downside deviation — more relevant than standard deviation)
- Beta to market (how much does portfolio move per 1% market move?)
- Correlation matrix (are positions truly diversified or just dressed up as diversified?)

### Concentration Risk
- Single position concentration limit
- Sector concentration limit
- Geographic concentration limit
- Factor concentration (are all positions exposed to the same macro factor?)
- Counterparty concentration (too much exposure to one prime broker, custodian, or dealer?)

### Liquidity Risk
- Time-to-liquidate each position at normal market volumes
- What % of portfolio can be liquidated in 1 day? 5 days? 30 days?
- Bid-ask spread under stress (can double or triple)
- Redemption pressure risk (for fund managers — investor outflow timing)

### Leverage & Margin
- Current gross and net leverage
- Margin utilization (how close to margin call?)
- Leverage ratio sensitivity to 10%, 20%, 30% drawdown
- Repo and financing haircut risk (haircuts widen in stress)
- Cross-default provisions in credit agreements

### Tail Risk
- Black swan scenarios and their portfolio impact
- Correlation spike risk (everything sells off together)
- Volatility spike risk (short vol positions)
- Currency crisis scenario
- Liquidity crisis scenario (cannot execute even at wide spreads)

## Phase 12 — Post-Action Monitoring

> After acting, define the surveillance system.

### Price-Based Tripwires
- Define specific price levels that trigger a mandatory reassessment
- Support and resistance levels in affected assets
- Vol regime change trigger (VIX above 30, 40?)
- Credit spread thresholds

### News-Based Tripwires
- Regulatory announcement expected
- Next earnings date
- Central bank meeting dates
- Economic data releases calendar (NFP, CPI, GDP)
- Counterparty credit rating watch

### Review Cadence
- Daily: price action, news scan, position P&L
- Weekly: macro data review, sentiment update, portfolio risk metrics
- Monthly: thesis validation, scenario probability update, position sizing review
- Quarterly: full strategic review — is the base case still intact?

### Documentation
- Record the thesis, the entry rationale, and the exit criteria at time of action
- Log actual vs expected outcome
- Conduct post-mortem on every significant decision (win or loss)
- Maintain a decision journal — the most underused risk management tool

## Special Situations Playbooks

### Bank Failure / Bank Stress
1. Check FDIC insurance coverage of deposits immediately
2. Assess contagion to counterparty banks
3. Monitor wholesale funding markets (CP, repo)
4. Watch for central bank emergency facilities (discount window, BTFP-type)
5. Assess duration risk in other banks' bond portfolios
6. Look for beneficiaries: short-term Treasuries, gold, systemic banks (flight to quality)

### Major Corporate Accident / Disaster
1. Liability insurance coverage analysis
2. Government/regulatory response speed
3. Business interruption duration estimate
4. Competitor benefit (market share gap)
5. Reputational damage permanence assessment
6. Activist or M&A implications post-distress

### Geopolitical Shock
1. Energy supply impact (oil, gas, food)
2. Safe-haven trade activation (USD, CHF, JPY, gold, Treasuries)
3. Defense sector beneficiaries
4. Supply chain disruption (semiconductor, rare earth dependencies)
5. Sanctions screening on all counterparties
6. Duration of risk premium (wars historically are shorter-risk events than recessions)

### Central Bank Policy Surprise
1. Recalculate equity discount rate impact immediately
2. Duration exposure impact on bond portfolio
3. Currency impact
4. Sector rotation implications (rate-sensitive sectors: utilities, REITs, financials)
5. FX carry trade unwind risk
6. Emerging market capital flow direction

### Credit Rating Downgrade
1. Forced seller analysis (who must sell? — index funds, insurance companies, pension funds)
2. Spread widening trajectory based on historical IG-to-HY downgrades
3. Refinancing cost impact
4. Covenant breach risk
5. Liquidity access impact
6. CDS market reaction

## Mental Models & Biases to Watch

### Useful Mental Models
- **Second-order thinking:** What happens after the obvious first effect?
- **Inversion:** What would have to be true for the bear case to play out fully?
- **Base rates:** How often does this type of event lead to systemic crisis vs containment?
- **Mr. Market:** Is the market being irrational today, and if so in which direction?
- **Margin of safety:** What is the price that makes this a good investment even if I'm partly wrong?
- **Circle of competence:** Do I actually understand this well enough to act, or am I outside my edge?
- **Reflexivity:** Is the market's reaction itself changing the fundamental outcome?

### Cognitive Biases to Audit
| Bias | How It Manifests | Antidote |
|---|---|---|
| Loss aversion | Holding losers too long | Pre-set exit rules before entry |
| Recency bias | Extrapolating last 6 months | Check 5Y, 10Y historical base rates |
| Anchoring | Stuck on old price target | Rebuild model from scratch, ignore prior price |
| Herding | Moving with consensus | Ask: what does everyone know that I don't? |
| Overconfidence | Oversizing high-conviction bets | Use Kelly sizing discipline |
| Confirmation bias | Seeking agreeable data | Actively seek out the bear case argument |
| Availability bias | Weighting recent/salient events too heavily | Use historical frequency data |
| Action bias | Feeling compelled to do something | "Don't just do something, stand there" |
| Narrative bias | Seduced by a compelling story | Stress-test the story against the numbers |
